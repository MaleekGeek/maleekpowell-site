You finally sit down.

The dishes are done. The kids are asleep. The house is quiet for the first time all day. And as soon as you get comfortable, you feel it. That little voice.

You should be doing something. Folding clothes. Answering that email. Getting a head start on tomorrow.

So you get back up.

If that's you, nothing's wrong with you. You learned a rule a long time ago, and it's still running in the background.

## The rule

It goes like this. Rest is something you earn. You work first, and if everything's done, maybe you get to sit down. Busy is good. Tired means lazy. Sitting still means you're wasting time.

A lot of us heard it growing up. "Don't let me catch you sitting down." "You can sleep when you're dead." People said it like a joke, and we took it seriously anyway.

For the people who raised us, it often made sense. Plenty of them didn't have the option to rest. A day off could mean a bill didn't get paid, so they taught us what kept them going.

Then phones took it further. You can answer emails from bed now. You can watch what everybody else is doing all day, and it always looks like more than you're doing. So the list never ends. And if the list never ends, you never get to stop.

## What it costs you

When rest has to be earned, you never actually get it, because there's always one more thing.

So you rest in pieces. Five minutes on your phone in the bathroom. A nap you feel bad about. A holiday where you check your email every morning.

And your body notices, even when you don't. You get short with the people you love. You wake up tired after a full night in bed.

## How to tell it's running your day

You apologise for sleeping in.

You explain why you're sitting down, even when nobody asked.

You feel like you have to deserve a day off.

You stay busy on purpose, because when you stop, feelings come up that you'd rather not deal with.

If that last one hit, go easy on yourself. And if what comes up when you stop feels heavy, talk to somebody you trust. You don't have to sit with it alone.

## Rest is part of the work

Athletes plan their rest days the same way they plan their training. Nobody calls them lazy for it. You're no different. You don't have to earn the sit-down. You need it so you can keep going.

And the people who love you don't need you running on empty. They need you here.

## Try this

This week, pick fifteen minutes and put it in your calendar like an appointment. Call it rest.

For those fifteen minutes, you don't fix anything, you don't scroll and you don't explain yourself. Sit outside. Lie down. Listen to one song all the way through.

When the guilt shows up, and it will, don't fight it. Notice it, and tell yourself, "That's the old rule talking." Then stay where you are.

The first time will feel strange. By the third or fourth time, it gets easier. You're teaching yourself a new rule.
